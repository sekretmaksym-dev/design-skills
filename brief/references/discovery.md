# Discovery: from "I want money" to a testable idea

Use in diagnose mode. Level 0 starts at step 1 and builds 5–7 ideas. Level 1 asks question 1, then 2 and 3 together, then 4 and 5, starts at step 2, and builds 3–5 ideas: the user's idea plus 2–4 angles on the same niche.

## 1. Five questions, one at a time

1. What can you do, or what do you know better than most people?
2. How many hours a week can you give this?
3. How much money can you spend before it earns anything?
4. Are you willing to talk to customers and sell directly?
5. How soon do you need the first money, and how much a month would make this worth it?

Answers narrow the options more than any trend does. Someone with two free evenings a week and someone with a team should get different ideas.

## 2. Filter the money models

Rough guides, not promises. Remove what does not fit the answers, and say why.

| Model | First money, roughly | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Service with lead capture | Weeks | A skill and willingness to sell |
| Fixed-scope service | Weeks | A repeatable service |
| Digital product, template, course | 1–3 months | An audience or a marketplace |
| Small tool or SaaS | 3–12 months | Building and support |
| Content with affiliates or ads | 6–12+ months | Patience and steady publishing |
| Directory or marketplace | Months | Supply on both sides |
| Physical store | Varies | Capital and logistics |

## 3. Gather evidence

Look for several signals of the same need:

- **People pay now:** competitors with prices, long-running ads, marketplace orders.
- **Interest is moving:** query trends, recent launches, fresh discussion.
- **Something hurts:** complaints on forums and in reviews. Bad reviews of a paid product are the strongest lead.
- **Room exists:** who holds the niche, and what they do badly.

A trend everyone is writing about is usually already crowded. Prefer a rising need with unhappy customers and weak incumbents.

## 4. Build the ideas

For each one record:

- what is sold and to whom, in one sentence;
- evidence of demand and of willingness to pay, with links;
- competitors and the open gap;
- the specific channel for the first ten customers;
- time to first money and starting budget;
- economics (step 5);
- fit with the five answers;
- confidence: high, medium, or low, and why.

Rank them by judgment and give the reasoning. Do not produce a weighted score: it suggests precision the data does not have.

## 5. Do the arithmetic

`visitors × conversion × price`, as a cautious case and a likely case. Conversion must appear as its own line, one line per step when the funnel has several (contact → call → paid), even if it is only an assumption: a bare "3 orders a month" hides it. Label each input with its source or as an assumption. Check that delivery hours leave time for finding clients, and give the result per hour of total work as well as per month. Convert currencies at the central bank's official rate for the date (for example the NBU for hryvnia), and record the rate and date. Compare the result with the income target from question 5. Often this alone shows that an idea cannot pay for itself. Say so.

## 6. Review and choose

Send the top three to the reviewers and revise. Then show them in the approval message (SKILL.md step 6), where the user chooses.

## 7. Plan the cheapest test

Pick the smallest thing that gets a real response:

- a one-page offer with a payment or a booking;
- 20 conversations with likely buyers;
- a marketplace listing;
- pre-orders or a paid pilot.

Sign-ups and compliments are weaker than money or a booked call. Set the pass mark and end date before the test starts, so the result cannot be argued with afterwards.
